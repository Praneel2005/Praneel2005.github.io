MK-DCSAU-Net: Segmentation-Guided Melanoma Classification via Multi-Kernel Artifact Removal

Split-attention deep neural network capturing scale variability and removing artifacts for dermoscopic diagnosis.

Published at: International Conference on Emerging Technologies and Computer Science (ICETCI 2026 - Springer Nature)
Authors: Sneha Chinchakandi, Yashaswini Mellikeri, Praneel Nadgir, Yashas A B, Sumaiya Pathan
Focus: Medical Image Segmentation, Split-Attention CNNs, Receptive Fields, CAD Systems
Links: ↓ PDF Paper

Abstract: One of the major dermoscopic melanoma classification problems is background noise; this is represented by hair, rulers, and gel residues, as well as the inability of the model to adapt to lesions of different sizes. To solve this, we suggest using MK-DCSAU-Net (Multi-Kernel Deeper and More Compact Split-Attention U-Net), which owns a Multi-Kernel Primary Feature Conservation (MK-PFC) module. The network is able to extract fine boundary details and global lesion structures by processing images with parallel $3\times3, 7\times7, \text{ and } 11\times11$ kernels.

Figure 1: MK-DCSAU-Net Pipeline. Diagram showing the five-level encoder-decoder structure. Standard input convolution is replaced by parallel Multi-Kernel PFC blocks with auxiliary supervised heads in the decoder.

Core Contributions

  • Multi-Kernel PFC Block: Directly addresses lesion scale variability by utilizing three parallel depthwise separable convolutions ($3\times3, 7\times7, 11\times11$) to extract multi-scale details.
  • Compact Split-Attention (CSA) Blocks: Suppresses background noise and accentuates diagnostically important structures through channel-wise feature recalibration.
  • Blackout Preprocessing: A mathematical bitwise mask operation ($I_{blackout} = I \odot M$) that sets all non-lesion pixels to zero, preventing the classifier from learning false correlations from hair or ruler marks.
  • Classification with Spatial Attention: Extends an EfficientNet-B3 backbone with a Spatial Attention Module (SAM) to isolate pigment networks under 8-way Test-Time Augmentation (TTA).

💡 High Boundary Fidelity & Gradient Stabilization

Deep supervision is integrated at intermediate decoder levels to stabilize gradient propagation during training on highly heterogeneous and class-imbalanced datasets. Combined with a mixed Dice BCE loss, it drastically improves segmentations on low-contrast lesions.
